# Cat-Game-Project-3
By Gia Linh Dang 

Puzzle design journal: 

The two puzzles I worked on were a narrative puzzle and a platformer puzzle. Both required me to think about how players interact with the game world, but the platformer puzzle challenged me more because I had to consider not only the puzzle's logic but also the player's timing, and ability to understand the environment. 

During the initial stage, I focused on the basic concept and how the player would progress through the level by thinking about it plainly as a platformer puzzle game. However, as I developed the narrative puzzle; the placement of obstacles, the distance between platforms, and the clarity of visual cues and context clues all affected how understandable and enjoyable the game was. I had to reconsider certain elements to ensure that the challenge was achievable without being too confusing or frustrating. 

Playtesting and repeated attempts helped me evaluate the puzzle from the player's perspective. Some were confused by the dialogue of the fish and a few were frustrated with the hitbox of the cat or the platform itself. When something did not work as intended, I needed to identify whether the problem came from the puzzle's design, the controls, or insufficient cues. This iterative process taught me the importance of testing ideas rather than assuming they would work as planned. Overall, creating this game taught me that game design is an iterative process involving experimentation, evaluation, and refinement. Each adjustment helped me better understand the relationship between the intended experience and the player's actual experience. In future projects, I would incorporate playtesting earlier and gather more feedback from other players to identify weaknesses and improve the overall design before I settle on something too early.
